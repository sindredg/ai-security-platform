This job is about the NetBox consumer contract. Read the evidence with get_evidence, one document at a time.
- ev-0001: the contract. Its accounts, the permissions it grants, the records each read may return, and the requests NetBox has to deny. A network part lists viewpoints: an address in a target subnet, and whether a connection from it should reach NetBox's port (open) or not (closed).
- ev-0002: the permissions configured for each account.
- ev-0003: the records each account's token returned for each read, and the status of each request the contract expects denied.
- ev-0004 and ev-0005, when ev-0001 has a network part: the security rules about NetBox's port in each target subnet, and what a connection from each viewpoint gave: open, timeout, refused, or error. In a subnet, the matching rule with the lowest priority decides, and no rule means denied. A connection reaches the port only when its subnet allows it out and NetBox's subnet allows it in.

Give each account one verdict, and each viewpoint one under viewpoint-NAME.
- excess: a configured permission grants an action on an object type the contract does not grant, or without its constraint; a token returned a record the contract does not list; NetBox allowed a request the contract expects denied; or a viewpoint the contract says is closed gets through by the rules or was open. Excess wins over missing.
- missing: a read the contract needs failed or returned less than it lists; no permission grants what the contract grants; or a viewpoint the contract says is open is stopped by the rules or timed out.
- within: what the contract allows, and nothing more.
- inconclusive: the evidence does not settle it, or ev-0002 and ev-0003, or ev-0004 and ev-0005, disagree.

For each excess, one change: for an account, the permission to take from it, from those ev-0002 lists for it; for a viewpoint, the inbound rule on NetBox's subnet in ev-0004 that lets it in, with the rule's priority where the schema asks for the permission's id.
